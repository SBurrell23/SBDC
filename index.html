/usr/share/readme.md

              

                

                  Hi! I'm Steven, a software architect and engineer. I design systems and
                  then build the software that runs on them — a decade of it on large scale
                  platforms in telecom, and now on assessment and qualification software at Pearson.
                  I have a Bachelors of Computer Science from Colorado State University.
                

                

                  I build agentically. My loop is spec, delegate, review, verify — I
                  direct coding agents the way I'd direct a team, which puts the distance between an
                  idea and a working version of it at hours instead of weeks. The home tab is the
                  receipt: most of what's there went from an empty repo to something you can play in
                  a day or two.
                

                

                  I care much less about a favorite stack than about choosing the right one and
                  getting the design honest first — data model, boundaries, failure modes.
                  The tools turn over every few months now. The thinking doesn't.

What I Do

            

              

                

                  

                  

                    
designSystems

                    

                      I start at the shape of the problem: requirements, data model, service
                      boundaries, failure modes, and the contracts between them. Most of what a
                      system will cost to run and change is decided before anyone writes a line,
                      so that is where the attention goes.
                    

                  

                

                

                  

                  

                    
shipWithAgents

                    

                      Spec, delegate, review, verify. I drive coding agents the way I'd drive a
                      team — tight specs, real acceptance criteria, and a review pass I
                      actually trust — and verify the result before it ships rather than
                      taking it on faith. It collapses weeks into days.
                    

                  

                

                

                  

                  

                    
prototypeToProve

                    

                      The fastest way to settle an argument about a product is to build the thing
                      and look at it. I turn vague ideas into working software quickly, so the
                      design conversation happens over something real instead of a mockup.
                    

                  

                

              


              

                

                  

                  

                    
buildFullStack

                    

                      Front to back, in whatever the problem calls for. Front ends in Vue, React,
                      Angular and vanilla JS; APIs and services in Node, Java, Python and PHP;
                      realtime over websockets and peer-to-peer. The language is an implementation
                      detail, the interfaces are not.
                    

                  

                

                

                  

                  

                    
modelData

                    

                      Choosing the right persistence layer and shaping the data around how it
                      actually gets read. I've worked across relational, key-value and document
                      stores — SQL, Redis, Couchbase, ElasticSearch, MongoDB.
                    

                  

                

                

                  

                  

                    
runItInProduction

                    

                      Software that has to stay up. I spent years on fault management for a
                      national network: realtime monitoring, alerting, and automation that had to
                      diagnose and act on its own rather than wake someone at 3am.
